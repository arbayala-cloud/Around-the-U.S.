Minute-by-minute account of Pike’s failed execution alarms medical experts

A minute-by-minute account from Pike’s lawyer, who was in the death chamber on Sept. 30, says the IV team took about six minutes on her right arm and nearly an hour on her left, while Pike grimaced as a needle was moved inside her arm. At 6:57 p.m. a team member said a vein “might be blowing”; at 7:07 another said “I think it’s blown”; at 7:23 saline pushed through the line “with difficulty” — and the procedure began a minute later. The team never tried the central line the protocol allows. Medical experts who reviewed the account called the IV work alarming and far below what any medical setting would accept. Tennessee’s executions remain paused through 2026, Pike’s own deadline fight still awaits a written ruling, and there is still no new execution date.

Postal Service’s election-mail push begins today, runs through Nov. 3

The Postal Service’s “extraordinary measures” for election mail begin today, Saturday, Oct. 24, and run through Election Day — longer in states with extended ballot-acceptance deadlines. The steps include extra deliveries and collections, special pickups, faster sort plans, and pulling ballots out of the regular mail stream for direct delivery to election offices. The agency’s standing advice: mail a completed ballot at least a week before your election office must receive it. Early voting in Texas ends Oct. 30.
