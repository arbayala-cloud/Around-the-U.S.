Minute-by-minute account of Pike’s failed execution alarms medical experts

Pike’s lawyer, who was in the death chamber on Sept. 30, says the IV team took about six minutes on her right arm and nearly an hour on her left, while Pike grimaced as a needle was moved inside her arm. At 6:57 p.m. a team member said a vein “might be blowing”; at 7:07 another said “I think it’s blown”; at 7:23 saline pushed through “with difficulty” — and the procedure began a minute later. The team never tried the central line the protocol allows. Medical experts who reviewed the account called the IV work alarming and far below what any medical setting would accept. Tennessee’s executions remain paused through 2026, Pike’s deadline fight still awaits a written ruling, and there is still no new execution date.

Brianna Maitland, 17, vanished after her shift — and 22 years later, still no answers

Seventeen-year-old Brianna Maitland finished her dishwashing shift at the Black Lantern Inn in Montgomery on the night of March 19, 2004, and never made it home. The next day her green 1985 Oldsmobile was found backed into an abandoned building about a mile from the restaurant, her uncashed paychecks still on the seat. Days passed before anyone realized she was missing. Investigators later came to believe the crash may have been staged, and police say the case points to foul play — but more than two decades later, it remains unsolved. The FBI offers up to $40,000 for information leading to her recovery.

Florida cold case cracked after nearly 20 years: two women charged in 2007 murder

Leon Heron, 34, was walking home from Taco Bell with dinner for his wife in Lauderdale Lakes on May 13, 2007, when two people tried to rob him — he fought back, and one of them shot him dead. The case went cold until April 2023, when a prison inmate came forward with information. Broward sheriff’s cold-case detectives say DNA places two women at the scene and both have confessed. Brittany Hanfield, 36, was arrested on a first-degree murder charge in November 2025 and has pleaded not guilty; a grand jury indicted Sheila Arstil, 37, on the same charge on Oct. 7.

Lummi Nation tribal member missing since January 2025; FBI offers $25,000 reward

Besalynn Mary James, who was 62 when she vanished, was last seen at 1:16 p.m. on Jan. 20, 2025, at her home on the Lummi Nation Indian Reservation in Bellingham, Washington, and was reported missing five days later. She is believed to have been wearing a black jacket; her belongings and her dogs — which she never left behind — stayed at home, and she did not drive. The FBI’s Seattle office, working with tribal and local police, is offering up to $25,000 for information leading to the arrest and conviction of those involved in her disappearance. Tips go to 1-800-CALL-FBI, tips.fbi.gov, or Lummi Nation Police at 360-312-2274.
